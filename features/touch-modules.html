Touch-Screen LCD Display Modules (Capacitive and Resistive)

A touch module is a panel with a touch sensor bonded to it. Capacitive (CTP) is the consumer-feel choice for bare-finger, multi-touch interfaces; resistive (RTP) keeps working with gloves, a stylus, or a wet screen, at the cost of a less premium feel and single-point touch. Several models below leave the choice selectable rather than fixing it - tell us which you need when you enquire.

When this is not the right spec

If your application does not need on-screen interaction at all, a non-touch panel is simpler, thinner and cheaper - touch is an added bonding step, not a default. See our full comparison of bonding methods before specifying either type.

Touch modules - questions

Capacitive or resistive - which do I need?

Capacitive for a consumer-feel, multi-touch UI operated bare-fingered; resistive when gloves, a stylus, or a wet or dusty screen are part of the use case. Our full CTP vs RTP guide goes through the trade-offs in detail.

What does “selectable” touch mean on a model page?

It means that base panel is bonded with either capacitive or resistive touch depending on your order - the glass and mechanical outline are the same either way, so telling us which type you need does not change lead time.

Do you bond touch panels in-house?

Yes - both air-gap and optical bonding, for capacitive and resistive touch, done in-house rather than outsourced. That is why changing the touch type or cover glass on an existing panel does not need a new supplier or a schedule surprise.
