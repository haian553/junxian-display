The factory behind the modules

1,500 m² production floor, 3 assembly lines, 19 machines and 52 staff including 6 engineers. Since 2005 we have done one thing in one building in Bao'an, Shenzhen: build display modules for other people's products. What started as monochrome LCDs for meters is now 188 models across 33 screen sizes - TFT, OLED, character, COG and custom segment glass - built by 52 people on 3 lines, forty minutes from Bao'an airport. This page is the plant, the story and the paperwork, photographed as it actually is.

What leaves this building, and who takes it

The product is the display module inside someone else's product: TFT and IPS colour panels, OLED modules, graphic LCD and character modules, and fully custom segment glass from 0.96" - 15.6", together with the FPC, backlight, touch panel and cover glass around them. The customers are equipment makers rather than distributors - industrial automation and HMI builders, instrument and metering manufacturers, energy-storage and EV-charging companies, handheld terminal and POS makers, appliance brands and medical device manufacturers. Several are listed companies in China; others are European and North American firms shipping a few thousand units a year.

We do not publish customer names, and we are suspicious of factories that do. Buyers who need references can ask during quotation and we will arrange what the customer in question has agreed to.

One distinction matters more than any number on this page: we are the manufacturer, not a trading company. When your enclosure does not fit or your MCU cannot drive the interface, the question is who can actually change something - and because the line is ours, a modified FPC or a different backlight current is a scheduling decision made downstairs, not a negotiation with someone else's factory.

What a plant this size is honestly good at

1,500 m² makes us a mid-sized module plant, and the honest version of what that buys you helps you decide faster than a page of superlatives.

Where we win

  • Small and mid-volume orders are routine. Runs of a few hundred to a few tens of thousands fit our line planning. Large panel fabs generally decline them.
  • Changeovers are quick. 3 lines and Yes in-house sampling mean a modified FPC does not wait behind a month of scheduled volume.
  • The people who quote you are in the same building as the line. Feasibility answers come from production, not from a sales database - usually the same day.

Where we do not

  • We do not make the glass. Almost no module house does. We buy TFT and OLED panels from panel makers and build the module: bonding, FPC, backlight, touch, driver, testing, packing. Custom segment glass is made to our order at partner glass lines.
  • We are not the cheapest at very high volume. At hundreds of thousands of units a year, a larger plant will beat us on unit price. Below that, our overheads and changeover speed put us ahead.

Follow an order through the building

Every module that ships makes the same journey. Glass, driver ICs, FPCs, LEDs and touch panels arrive and are checked against the approved specification before they touch the line. The driver IC is bonded to the glass - COG or COF, with alignment and bonding-strength checks - then the FPC goes on, the backlight is built and fitted, and the module is framed. Where the order specifies touch, the sensor and cover glass are bonded, air-gap or optical, in a controlled environment that keeps particles out of the stack.

Then every single module is powered on and inspected - dead pixels, mura, backlight uniformity, contrast, interface response - before spending time on the ageing racks and passing a final sampling inspection against the approved reference. Packing is Anti-static bag + foam tray + export carton: anti-static protection is not optional on driver-bonded modules. The photographs below are those steps on our own line.

How we behave when you order

Before the order. Technical questions get technical answers. If a standard model does the job, we say so rather than quoting custom tooling.

During production. The approved sample is the reference. Changes to glass, driver IC or backlight on a running project are notified in writing before they happen.

After delivery. Engineering support for integration problems - interface timing, initialisation code, mechanical fit. Most of these are solved in a day by email.
