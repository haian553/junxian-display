RGB Parallel LCD Display Modules

Your processor drives the panel directly - 16, 18 or 24 data lines plus sync signals, straight out of the display controller on your SoC. There is no frame buffer on the panel to manage. Your host streams every pixel of every frame, which is what makes the timing predictable and the panel cheap.

When RGB Parallel is not the right choice

Skip RGB if your MCU has no display controller, or if you cannot spare the pins - it wants sixteen to twenty-plus of them. Driving it from a controller-less MCU means adding a bridge IC, and that is an extra cost and an extra failure point rather than a saving. Look at SPI or a smart serial module instead.

RGB Parallel - questions

How do I know if my processor has an RGB/DPI controller?

Check the datasheet for an LCD, DPI or parallel display controller block - if the SoC has one, it is usually the cheapest way to drive a colour panel above small sizes. If it does not, SPI, MCU-parallel or a smart serial module are the alternatives.

How many pins does an RGB interface actually use?

Typically 16 to 24 data lines (depending on colour depth) plus HSYNC, VSYNC, pixel clock and data enable - more than SPI or MCU-parallel, which is the trade-off for higher bandwidth without an on-panel frame buffer.

Can you bond touch and cover glass to an RGB panel?

Yes - most of our touch category is RGB or MCU-parallel TFT with capacitive or resistive touch bonded on top; the interface and the touch bonding are independent decisions.
