Cover Glass and Surface Treatment: AG, AF, AR and Printing Options

The surface finish on a cover glass changes how a display reads in the real world as much as the panel underneath it does, and it is one of the most common places a customisation request starts once a buyer has already chosen a panel.

Written by the Junxian applications engineering team. Figures quoted for “our models” are calculated from the 103 models on this site that carry a full parameter set, so they change when the catalogue changes.

Why the surface finish matters as much as the glass

A cover glass with no treatment is a mirror as much as it is a window - specular reflections from overhead lighting or direct sun can wash out even a bright panel underneath. Surface treatment is a separate decision from panel brightness: a well-chosen coating can do more for real-world legibility than pushing the backlight higher, and it is nearly always cheaper.

Anti-glare, anti-reflective and anti-fingerprint compared

TreatmentWhat it does Trade-off
Anti-glare (AG)A matte etched surface that diffuses reflections rather than concentrating them into a bright spotSlightly softens image clarity compared with a glossy surface
Anti-reflective (AR)A multi-layer optical coating that reduces the amount of light reflected, keeping the surface glossy and clearCosts more than AG; less effective than AG against a single strong direct light source
Anti-fingerprint (AF)An oleophobic coating that resists skin oils and makes the surface easier to wipe cleanUsually combined with AG or AR rather than used alone

The practical rule: AG for equipment under mixed or overhead lighting where a single bright reflection is the problem; AR where image clarity has to stay high and reflections are more diffuse; AF added to either when the display is touched by bare hands.

Printing, logo windows and colour-matched bezels

Cover glass can carry printed graphics, an opaque border, a cut-out logo window, or a colour-matched bezel edge - all done on the same glass as the touch or protective layer, not as a separate part bonded on afterward. Send us the artwork and the areas that need to stay clear and we will confirm minimum feature size and registration tolerance for your specific glass thickness.

Bonding method interacts with the surface choice

How the cover glass is bonded to the panel changes what the surface treatment is actually fighting - an air-gap bonded stack has an internal reflective interface that optical bonding removes, which is why an optically bonded panel often needs less aggressive surface treatment to read well in bright light. See our bonding methods guide for the full comparison before specifying both together.

Questions engineers ask us about this

Can I add anti-glare to an existing panel I already ordered?

Yes - cover glass and surface treatment are changed independently of the panel underneath, so this is one of the more common modification requests on a repeat order.

Does anti-glare reduce touch sensitivity?

No - the treatment is on the outer surface of the cover glass, above the touch sensor layer, and does not affect capacitive or resistive touch performance.

What is the minimum order for a custom cover glass?

Cover glass changes are usually within our custom tooling tier - 1,000 pcs - though a treatment or printing change on an existing outline can sometimes be quoted lower. Send the specification and we will confirm.

Can you colour-match a bezel to our brand colour?

Yes - send a Pantone reference or a physical sample and we will confirm the closest achievable match before tooling.

Need a quote or a datasheet?

Send us size, resolution, interface and annual quantity. Our engineers reply within one working day. Sample MOQ 5 pcs, lead time 10-20 days.
