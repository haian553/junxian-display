Display Module Glossary

52 terms used across this site and in LCD, OLED and touch module datasheets generally - glass modes, interfaces, touch and bonding, and the commercial terms that come up in a quotation.

Glass modes and optics

TN
Twisted Nematic - the simplest and lowest-cost LCD glass mode. Good contrast viewed straight-on, narrows quickly off-axis. Glass mode guide →
IPS
In-Plane Switching - a TFT glass mode with wide, stable viewing angles and colour that does not shift off-axis, at a higher cost than TN. Glass mode guide →
VA
Vertical Alignment - a glass mode giving the deepest black and highest contrast of the common TFT modes, with a viewing angle between TN and IPS. Glass mode guide →
STN
Super-Twisted Nematic - a passive-matrix glass mode used on monochrome and character displays, lower cost than active-matrix TFT. COG modules →
FSTN
Film-compensated STN - an STN variant with an added retarder film that corrects the colour tint STN otherwise shows, giving a neutral black-on-white or blue-on-white image.
HTN
High-Twisted Nematic - a passive glass mode between TN and STN, giving better contrast across temperature than TN on segment and character displays. Segment LCD guide →
VATN
Vertical-Aligned TN - the highest-contrast, widest-viewing-angle passive glass mode we offer for custom segment displays, used where legibility cannot slip. Segment LCD guide →
Transflective
A display type that reflects ambient light like a reflective panel in bright conditions and uses a backlight like a transmissive panel in low light - readable both outdoors and in the dark on the same panel. Brightness guide →
Nits / cd/m²
Units of surface luminance - one nit equals one candela per square metre. The brightness figure on every datasheet is measured this way, typically at full backlight drive and 25 °C. Brightness guide →
Contrast ratio
The luminance ratio between a panel’s brightest white and darkest black under stated ambient light conditions - meaningful mainly when the ambient condition is also stated.
Viewing angle
The angle off-centre at which contrast has dropped to a stated threshold (commonly 10:1). Wider is better for a display read from more than one position. Glass mode guide →
View direction
The clock-position (commonly 6 o’clock or 12 o’clock) that gives the best contrast on a passive display - specified opposite to where the operator actually stands relative to the glass.
Gray scale inversion direction
On passive and some active glass, image contrast can invert when viewed from the opposite direction to the one it was designed for - a standard datasheet field, not a defect.
Response time
How quickly a pixel switches between grey levels, usually quoted in milliseconds. Matters for fast-moving content; largely irrelevant for static instrument UIs.
Duty ratio
On a passive-matrix (non-active) display, the fraction of time each segment line is actually driven, for example 1/4 or 1/16. Fewer segments per line (lower ratio) gives better contrast at the cost of more drive pins. Segment LCD guide →

Interfaces and electrical

SPI
Serial Peripheral Interface - a 4-5 wire serial bus where the panel’s own driver IC holds the frame buffer, giving the lowest pin count of any interface that still drives colour graphics. SPI interface page →
MCU interface (8080 / 6800)
An 8 or 16-bit parallel bus with read/write/chip-select control lines, named after the classic microcontroller bus timing conventions it follows. MCU interface page →
RGB (parallel)
A direct parallel pixel bus - typically 16 to 24 data lines plus sync signals - driven from a display controller on the host processor, with no frame buffer on the panel itself. RGB interface page →
MIPI DSI
Mobile Industry Processor Interface, Display Serial Interface - a high-speed differential serial interface used by most modern application processors, needing far fewer physical pins than RGB at the same resolution. MIPI interface page →
LVDS
Low-Voltage Differential Signalling - a parallel-to-serial interface used on larger panels and longer cable runs than RGB comfortably supports. Interface guide →
I²C
A 2-wire serial bus (SDA/SCL) shared with other devices - the lowest pin count of any display interface, at the lowest bandwidth, mainly used on small OLED and monochrome panels. I2C interface page →
UART / smart serial
A display with its own on-board controller that takes text and value commands over a simple serial link, rather than streaming pixels - removes the graphics stack from the host firmware. UART interface page →
Driver IC
The controller chip that converts interface commands or pixel data into the analogue voltages that actually drive the glass - for example ST7789, GC9A01 or ST7066U. Driver IC guide →
VCOM
A per-glass analogue voltage setting on the driver IC that has to match the specific panel it is paired with - copying VCOM from a different module’s example code is a common cause of washed-out or ghosting images. White screen troubleshooting →
Logic voltage
The supply voltage for the driver IC’s digital interface logic, kept separate from the backlight LED supply and, on some panels, from the analogue panel drive voltage.
PPI
Pixels per inch - a display’s pixel density, calculated from resolution and diagonal size. Higher PPI means finer detail is resolvable at the same viewing distance. Choosing a module guide →
Dot pitch
The physical centre-to-centre distance between adjacent pixels or dots, usually given in millimetres - the mechanical counterpart to PPI.
PWM dimming
A backlight dimming method that switches the LEDs fully on and off faster than the eye can follow, varying the ratio of on-time to off-time rather than the current itself - keeps colour more consistent across the dimming range than analogue current dimming. Backlight lifetime guide →
L50 / backlight lifetime
The number of hours of continuous operation, at rated drive current and a stated temperature, before an LED backlight’s brightness decays to 50% of its initial value. Backlight lifetime guide →

Touch, cover glass and structure

CTP
Capacitive Touch Panel - projected-capacitive touch sensing, bare-finger and multi-touch capable, the consumer-feel default. CTP vs RTP guide →
RTP
Resistive Touch Panel - pressure-sensed touch that keeps working with gloves, a stylus, or a wet screen, at the cost of single-point touch and a less premium feel. CTP vs RTP guide →
Air-gap bonding
Mounting a touch panel or cover glass over the display with a thin air layer between them - cheaper and independently repairable, but the air interface reflects some light and reduces outdoor contrast. CTP vs RTP guide →
Optical bonding
Filling the gap between touch panel, cover glass and display with optical adhesive - removes the reflective air interface, improving sunlight readability and reducing internal condensation risk. CTP vs RTP guide →
OCA
Optical Clear Adhesive - the transparent adhesive film most commonly used for optical bonding between display, touch sensor and cover glass layers. CTP vs RTP guide →
AG (anti-glare)
A matte, etched cover glass surface that diffuses reflections instead of concentrating them into a bright spot, at a small cost to image sharpness. Cover glass guide →
AR (anti-reflective)
A multi-layer optical coating that reduces the amount of light reflected off the cover glass while keeping the surface glossy and clear - costs more than AG. Cover glass guide →
AF (anti-fingerprint)
An oleophobic coating that resists skin oils and makes a touch surface easier to wipe clean, usually combined with AG or AR. Cover glass guide →
Active area
The actual visible, addressable image area of a panel - smaller than the module outline dimension by the width of the surrounding bezel and seal.
Outline dimension
The full physical outer size of the module, including the bezel - what has to fit inside your enclosure cutout.
FPC
Flexible Printed Circuit - the flexible ribbon connecting the glass to a connector or directly to your board; length, exit direction and connector position are all customisable per project.
COG
Chip-On-Glass - a construction where the driver IC is bonded directly onto the glass itself rather than onto a separate FPC, giving a compact, low-profile module. COG category →
COB
Chip-On-Board - a construction where the driver IC is bonded onto a small PCB attached to the glass, common on character LCD modules. Character LCD category →

Manufacturing and commercial

MOQ
Minimum Order Quantity - the smallest quantity we will produce or supply at a given price tier. Ours: 5 pcs samples, 100 pcs standard catalogue items, 1,000 pcs where new tooling is required. MOQ and lead time guide →
NRE
Non-Recurring Engineering - the one-off cost of tooling, design or qualification work for a custom part, quoted separately from the per-piece production price so you can see what is one-off. Custom development guide →
Tooling
The mould, glass mask or fixture set built specifically for a custom part - required whenever glass size, shape or segment layout changes from an existing design. Custom development guide →
Approved sample
The physical sample a buyer signs off before mass production - it becomes the binding reference for dimensions, display quality and interface timing on every repeat order. MOQ and lead time guide →
EOL
End-of-Life - the point at which a component is discontinued by its original manufacturer. Requires a requalification decision, not just a purchasing one. End-of-life guide →
EXW / FOB / CIF
Incoterms defining where cost and risk transfer from seller to buyer: EXW at our factory door, FOB once loaded at the departure port, CIF including freight and insurance to the destination port. HS code and duties guide →
HS code
Harmonized System code - the international customs classification number used to determine import duty and documentation requirements, extended into country-specific tariff schedules by each importing market. HS code and duties guide →
HMI
Human-Machine Interface - the operator-facing display and control panel on industrial or instrument equipment. Industrial HMI industry page →
BMS
Battery Management System - in this context, the electronics that monitor and protect a battery pack, often paired with a smart serial display module that renders its status without needing its own graphics stack. Energy storage industry page →
ESD
Electrostatic Discharge - a risk factor in handling and shipping bare glass and assembled modules, managed with anti-static bags, trays and ESD-protected workstations throughout packing. Packaging and ESD guide →
